### Five different “confidence” and success numbers

1. **Win probability:** the moneyline model's `P(home wins)` for one game; evaluate its Brier score, log loss and calibration against final results (exclude ties or state a tie policy). Picking the larger probability and getting the winner right is **hit rate**, not calibration.
2. **Evidence confidence:** `confidence-v1` is a **0–100 diagnostic**, with data/model/market weights **35/35/30**; model evidence mixes revision stability and retained error, market strength includes the *absolute* model–market gap, freshness and two-book agreement. Missing/failed gates cap the score ([framework](../artifacts/api-server/src/lib/confidence-framework.ts)). **70/100 does not mean a 70% win chance, and a larger gap does not prove value.** Its historical-tier helper currently reports insufficient sample, not an empirically validated reliability percentage.
3. **Pick coverage:** `number of eligible scheduled games with a frozen official pick by the predeclared cutoff / all scheduled games in the specified slate`, plus a separate denominator restricted to games with required market evidence. A game with no quote, no vector, missed freeze, late snapshot or cancelled kickoff must retain its reason; never quietly remove it to raise coverage.
4. **Hit rate:** correct official chosen winners / official graded winner picks with legitimate final score; show numerator, denominator, ties/voids, season/week and uncertainty. **Grading completion** = graded / frozen official picks with final outcomes; report pending separately. No-picks are not losses and do not vanish from coverage.
5. **Price-aware betting results:** only when an *actual, timestamped, available-at-decision* selection/price and settlement rules are retained: wins/losses/pushes/voids, units staked, net units and ROI = net units / staked units. Compare implied probabilities after removing vig when appropriate. The 2025 recorded-line simulation had spread **224 / −4.03%**, moneyline **277 / −3.58%**, totals **182 / −11.73%** at a fixed **research** rule; it is neither a live record nor threshold advice. True CLV needs matched timestamped entry and closing quotes at the same book/selection; it remains unavailable ([market study](gridline-2025-market-baseline.md)). Existing proposed **#45** addresses price-aware reporting.

## Ranked, testable research sequence

Do **not** choose a new algorithm or a betting threshold from the current first slate. Each stage freezes its protocol *before* seeing its evaluation outcomes, reports counts and exclusions, and leaves the approved model untouched.

1. **Establish the comparison register first.** Define per-family candidate universe by season/week, version, observation cutoff, verified provider availability and final status. Compare official model with a prior-games team-form/simple rating baseline, logistic home-field/base-rate moneyline, league/rolling totals and, on the *same eligible games*, contemporaneously observed de-vigged market probabilities and quoted spread/total when actually available. For player families compare same-player last-3/last-5/season means; TD against position and prior-player rates. **Pass to next stage only if** every metric has numerator/denominator, cutoff, source age, unmatched/excluded counts and an identically indexed baseline; null market evidence stays null. The 2025 recorded-market study is a separate historical diagnostic, not the contemporaneous benchmark.
2. **Prospective, chronological shadow register.** Log immutable candidate and incumbent forecasts at the same predeclared cutoff and feature policy *before kickoff*, then append final outcomes; evaluate paired loss differences week by week, season by season and by low-sample/freshness strata. Keep training ≤ prior seasons, calibration fitted only on earlier seasons, and untouched later-season/forward shadow; never choose hyperparameters using the holdout and then report that holdout as untouched. **Success:** exact game/player pairing and audit of every missing/late row; a candidate's improvement over simple and market baselines persists across at least two forward evaluation windows with a predeclared confidence interval that excludes no improvement, not merely one lucky week. If windows or market matches are absent, **inconclusive**.
3. **Probability calibration, then abstention.** For moneyline and TD probability (separately), report Brier, log loss, calibration intercept/slope, reliability buckets with n and uncertainty; compare uncalibrated vs train-only Platt/isotonic calibration on untouched later games. Plot selective risk against coverage for rules based only on pregame quality/freshness, with abstentions counted in the full slate denominator and outcomes never used to decide abstention. **Success:** lower paired held-out proper score without material subgroup degradation and a predeclared coverage floor; otherwise retain the simple probability or no pick. No 70%+ claim from n=6 or n=3.
4. **Quantify uncertainty.** Use game/week- or season-blocked paired bootstrap intervals for metric deltas (not independent per-play resampling), Wilson intervals for hit rate, and empirically validated coverage of residual prediction intervals for margins, totals and player counts. Distinguish uncertainty in *average performance* from uncertainty for *one game*. **Success:** predeclared intervals attain nominal coverage in an untouched forward window with subgroup counts disclosed; small samples yield “insufficient,” not zero-width certainty.
5. **Only then test candidates.** Start with ridge/logistic and simple calibrated baselines; test totals boosting against ridge on exactly the same low-sample policy. Later try shrinkage/opponent-adjusted form, QB-availability features or carefully regularized ensembles only after archived point-in-time identity, injuries and publication chronology pass. Position-adjusted players and red-zone TD additions require paired archive coverage and a measured ablation benefit. **Success:** predeclared improvement in paired MAE for points/counts or Brier/log loss for probabilities with stable forward results, adequate coverage and no regression in leakage/freshness gates. Never promote automatically. Existing proposed **#46** addresses realistic cutoff regression tests; **#44** scales reporting, not evidence quality.

Report first-slate intervals and reasons as operational diagnostics; with at most 15 prospective games, postpone accuracy, calibration and ROI conclusions regardless of the observed W–L.
