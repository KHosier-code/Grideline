# Gridline defense-vs-position source assessment

**Assessment date:** September 26, 2026. **Disposition:** The existing nflverse player-game statistics and schedule-backed team identity can support a descriptive, retrospective “production allowed by opposing position” view, subject to complete-game/source-coverage gates. It cannot support a named defender matchup, and the currently persisted source data cannot replay exactly what was published and known at an arbitrary historical pregame cutoff. `ffopportunity` is an optional expected-fantasy-points/opportunity enrichment, not a defense-vs-position feed. This is source qualification only: no model, UI, schema, import, database, or provider changes were made.

## Definition: team defense versus position is not defender versus player

For a completed game, “defense allowed to QB/RB/WR/TE” should mean the aggregate recorded offensive production of the opposing players whose documented primary position is that position, grouped to one opposing defensive team and one canonical game. The unit of observation and denominator is a **covered defensive game**, not a player row. For example, three WR stat lines against one defense contribute to the WR totals for one game, not three defensive games. Per-game averages divide by distinct completed defensive games whose relevant source coverage is verified; partial or absent source rows must not be silently converted to zero.

This descriptive team-level grouping does not identify which defender covered a player, distinguish man from zone, or attribute shared/switch coverage. It also does not predict the upcoming game. If “WR1” is used, its ranking rule and observation window must be explicit; it is a receiver-role proxy, not a persistent player/CB pairing.

For the requested broad view, the existing player-game fields map naturally to these kinds of descriptive totals: QB passing attempts/completions/yards/TD/interceptions and separately rushing; RB carries/rushing yards/rushing TD and receiving targets/receptions/yards/TD; WR and TE targets/receptions/receiving yards/TD. Only fields actually present and source-covered should be offered. Keep season and postseason separate, report season/weeks/game coverage and source freshness, and treat red-zone measures as a separate metric with their own coverage. These definitions do not establish that every metric is currently complete for every game.

## External research

### Open Source Football’s WR1 article: useful retrospective method, not pregame evidence

Jonas Trostle’s [“Evauluating defenses by how well they play the offense’s WR 1”](https://opensourcefootball.com/posts/2021-07-30-evauluating-defenses-by-how-well-they-play-the-offenses-wr-1/) (published July 29, 2021) explicitly opens by saying cornerback performance is hard to measure. Its example downloads nflfastR play-by-play for 1999–2019, joins receiver IDs to roster GSIS IDs, and ranks each team’s WRs **within each year** by the sum of targeted air yards. It then labels rank 1 as WR1 and compares mean EPA on plays credited to that receiver with mean EPA against other receivers, grouped by defense and year. The author notes alternative metrics (QB EPA, CPOE, totals, targets, receptions) produce different results. For the historical coverage-score analysis, the article switches to total yards because targeted air yards do not extend far enough back.

This is a transparent example of a *season-level receiver-role split*, not evidence of a named corner assignment or a weekly pregame matchup. Its WR1 rank uses the whole season’s targeted air yards, including production later than any hypothetical in-season cutoff; using that rank for a historical pregame feature would leak future information. The play-level receiver/defense aggregation cannot show a particular CB shadowed the WR, and a targeted receiver is not proof of exclusive coverage responsibility. The article’s text and figures are marked [CC BY-NC 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/); do not copy its figures/text or assume commercial reuse rights from its publication.

### `ffopportunity` and its nflreadr/nflreadpy loaders

The [ffopportunity project](https://ffopportunity.ffverse.com/) describes itself as models and data for Expected Fantasy Points (EP): it applies an XGBoost model to nflverse play-by-play to quantify expected fantasy points for a player’s opportunity/situation. Its documentation describes training on public nflverse play-by-play from 2006–2020. It is **not** a dataset of defensive assignments, opponent-position rankings, corner coverage, or an independent feed of actual season totals.

The [nflreadr loader reference](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/reference/load_ff_opportunity.html) exposes `load_ff_opportunity(seasons, stat_type, model_version)` with `stat_type` values `weekly`, `pbp_pass`, and `pbp_rush`, and model versions `latest` or `v1.0.0`. It returns precomputed expected-points data from ffopportunity’s automated releases. The [ffopportunity data dictionary](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/articles/dictionary_ff_opportunity.html) covers separate `weekly`, `pbp_pass`, and `pbp_rush` tables. Documented identifiers/attributes include `game_id`, `play_id` (with game and drive needed for a unique play), `posteam`, position, player IDs and names; the loader’s `pbp_pass` example shows passer and receiver IDs, positions, pass attempts, receiving yards, touchdowns, and other play facts. These fields can help organize **offensive player opportunities by game**, but the model-derived EP columns should not be relabeled “defense allowed” without a separately specified aggregation and validation.

The project’s [license page](https://ffopportunity.ffverse.com/) distinguishes **GPL v3 for the R code** from **CC BY-SA 4.0 for the models and expected-points data**. The latter requires attribution and ShareAlike treatment for adaptations; confirm the intended product use and attribution with counsel before shipping derived data. The [Open Source Football article’s CC BY-NC license](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/) is a separate license and does not apply to ffopportunity.

**Version and cadence observed on September 26, 2026:**

- The [ffopportunity releases page](https://github.com/ffverse/ffopportunity/releases) has a moving `latest-data` release and a separate model release; the `latest-data` release record was updated **2026-09-25 10:34:40 UTC** ([release API record](https://api.github.com/repos/ffverse/ffopportunity/releases/tags/latest-data)). The package source [DESCRIPTION](https://github.com/ffverse/ffopportunity/blob/main/DESCRIPTION) says package version **0.1.2**.
- Its documented `.github/workflows/ep-update-data.yaml` [workflow](https://github.com/ffverse/ffopportunity/blob/main/.github/workflows/ep-update-data.yaml) schedules data refresh attempts on selected Sunday, Monday, Tuesday and Thursday windows in months 1, 2 and 9–12, with manual dispatch also enabled. This is a scheduled automation, not a guaranteed publication SLA; actual successful upload and data availability may lag.
- The [nflreadr release list](https://github.com/nflverse/nflreadr/releases) showed **1.5.1** as newest (released April 20, 2026); its [loader implementation](https://github.com/nflverse/nflreadr/blob/main/R/load_ffverse.R) builds URLs from the requested season/type and model-version release tag. `latest` is a moving pointer, not an immutable historical version.
- The [nflreadpy release list](https://github.com/nflverse/nflreadpy/releases) showed **v0.1.5** as newest (November 19, 2025). Its [repository documentation](https://github.com/nflverse/nflreadpy) lists `load_ff_opportunity` among the available loaders and describes Polars results. The Python library is a loader/client, not a different underlying source or an as-of archive.

For context, nflreadr’s [data schedule documentation](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/articles/nflverse_data_schedule.html) says player/team stats use the play-by-play update schedule: nightly after game days and at additional points on game days during the season. Its PBP section says the automated public feed is updated nightly after game days (and at additional points on game days), with NFL stat corrections incorporated by Wednesday and a Wednesday-night/Thursday refresh recommended as the cleanest version. This is a release-latency/correction warning, not assurance that an earlier game’s statistics were available at kickoff of the next game.

## What Gridline persists today

Inspection of `lib/db/src/schema/nfl.ts`, `artifacts/api-server/src/lib/nflverse.ts`, and the existing player-use/red-zone reports establishes these current source boundaries:

| Persisted object / process | Relevant fields and keys | What its provenance does—and does not—prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `games` | Canonical `game_id` PK; season, week, game date, optional kickoff, canonical home/away team IDs, status and scores. | A source game should be reconciled to a final canonical game; NFLverse source game IDs are not the same identifier as the canonical schedule IDs. |
| `player_game_stats` | Player ID/name, position, source team/opponent abbreviations, season/week/season type, passing/rushing/receiving measures and `source_updated_at`. Unique key is player + season + week + season type + opponent; **there is no `game_id` column**. | `parsePlayerStatsRow` accepts the source player ID and stats, and `ingestPlayerStats` upserts by the unique key. The upsert sets `sourceUpdatedAt` to Gridline’s current time. It is an import/update time, not the source file’s original publication time. Use unique GSIS player IDs and canonical team/game reconciliation, not name-only matching. |
| `nflverse_source_files` | One row per dataset/season; source URL/local cache path, status, byte size, rows processed, started/completed times and error. | The ledger is mutable per dataset-season and has no required checksum, immutable provider release ID, source-published-at value, or retained prior payload/version. Status/row count are useful import evidence, not a source-content snapshot. |
| `data_sync_runs` | Provider, status, job key, scheduled/start/end times, processed-record count, error/skip reason and optional JSON metadata. | This is a run ledger, not a per-row history or guaranteed upstream publication-time ledger. Its processed-record count is not necessarily a count of inserted player-game rows. |
| Red-zone facts | Player/team per-game facts have canonical game ID plus source game ID, season/week/type, player/team/opponent, zone, counts, ingestion time and nullable source-updated time. | These are derived PBP facts, not a general DvP table. The documented derivation sets upstream `sourceUpdatedAt` to null when it is not supplied and stores local `ingestedAt` separately. Its reports explicitly require missing weeks to remain partial/unavailable, not zeros. |
| PBP ingestion | Existing nflverse process streams PBP into persisted team-game and QB aggregates and derives selected red-zone facts. | The existing work log says individual PBP rows are **not persisted**. Therefore Gridline does not retain the complete underlying historical play table needed to replay arbitrary prior views. |

The same source picture is documented in [`gridline-red-zone-opportunity-work-log.md`](gridline-red-zone-opportunity-work-log.md), [`gridline-player-usage-production-verification.md`](gridline-player-usage-production-verification.md), and [`gridline-player-projection-baseline.md`](gridline-player-projection-baseline.md). The usage verification records a 2026 `player_stats` file ledger row and same-sized current download, but explicitly notes the original imported bytes were not retained with a trusted checksum and that a current re-download does not prove exact historical value equality. The historical projection baseline likewise states source publication times were not independently archived.

The existing development importer uses the season-specific public nflverse release URL, streams the file, parses stable player identifiers/stat fields and updates the file ledger; see [`nflverse.ts`](../artifacts/api-server/src/lib/nflverse.ts). For 2025 onward it selects the `stats_player/stats_player_week_<season>.csv.gz` path. The imported `player_game_stats` key joins through season/week and opposing teams to the canonical schedule; PBP’s source game IDs require a separate season/week/team mapping. Apply existing canonical team aliases (including nflverse `WAS` to Gridline/schedule `WSH`) before matching. The player identity source/crosswalk must resolve uniquely; unresolved player IDs or duplicate/ambiguous schedule matches are excluded and counted, not repaired by matching names.

### Rights for the actual Gridline source

The [nflverse-data repository license](https://github.com/nflverse/nflverse-data/blob/main/LICENSE.md) identifies the general repository data license as **CC BY 4.0**. This does not override a component’s separately identified source terms. For example, the [nflreadr 1.5.0 release notes](https://github.com/nflverse/nflreadr/releases/tag/v1.5.0) state FTN-sourced participation from 2023 onward is **CC BY-SA 4.0** and requires credit to FTN Data via nflverse; that is a separate dataset, not proof that every `player_stats` row or PBP field has those FTN terms. The requested DvP should not use participation data: nflreadr’s schedule page documents its post-season publication timing, so it cannot evidence a current-season pregame player matchup. Record the exact selected dataset/source and honor its attribution/license, rather than inferring a blanket grant from the loader package’s MIT license.

## Timing and historical replay decision

**Outcome analysis of already completed games is feasible from present persisted stats where the rows and game coverage are verified. The existing Gridline import alone cannot reconstruct the exact release that existed before a historic game.** A [2026 public archive asset and row audit](gridline-player-td-archive-2026-audit.md) found SHA-256-verified weekly player-stat/PBP pairs available before all Week 2 and Week 3 kickoffs; a [feature-contract follow-up](gridline-player-td-feature-contract-provenance-followup.md) corrected the initially overstrict league-wide completeness interpretation. The model only reads relevant player/team/opponent prior games. A verified Sep 3 prior-season pair also exists before the first Week 1 kickoff. These are source-timing candidates, not approval: 2021–2025 training chronology, feature derivations and live availability remain unresolved.

Reasons:

1. nflverse player stats and PBP are updated after games and may be corrected later in the week. An NFL game date alone cannot prove a row was public before a different game’s kickoff.
2. `player_game_stats.source_updated_at` is set by Gridline’s import/upsert and may be reset when a file is reimported. It does not preserve the original upstream publication timestamp or earlier versions of a corrected row.
3. The current source-file ledger tracks a single mutable dataset/season record, file size and counts, but not an immutable source hash/version or prior source bytes. `data_sync_runs` records runs, not exact row-level historical states.
4. The stored player-game stats omit `game_id`, and source play rows are not retained. Reconciliation to the canonical schedule can be performed with season/week/teams, but cannot restore an old source payload that Gridline did not archive.
5. `ffopportunity`’s `latest-data` tag and `model_version="latest"` move over time; the release documentation does not promise an immutable series of publication-time snapshots suitable for pregame backtesting. Its generated data timestamps describe generation, not a version archive for every previous pregame cutoff.
6. The WR1 article ranks receivers on full-season targeted air yards. That retrospective role classification is intrinsically unavailable before the season is complete and is future-leaking if used in an earlier-week replay.

The forward-looking use is different: for a future game, use only already completed, source-covered games and require their **Gridline capture time** and any independently known source publication time to precede `min(request time, kickoff - 1 ms)`. Current imported rows may support that future view once the upstream release has actually arrived; the captured historical database does not establish the same row state existed at earlier cutoffs. If a historical pregame reconstruction is a product/research requirement, begin retaining immutable source bytes or content hashes, source release/version and publication/update timestamps, capture timestamps, and corrected/superseded row snapshots before claiming point-in-time reproducibility.

## Qualification matrix and exclusions

| Candidate | Qualified use | Exclusions / unresolved gates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gridline `player_game_stats` from nflverse player stats | Primary current source for descriptive season/week/team/opponent position production where position/player IDs, final schedule joins and source coverage are validated. Group player production first to a canonical defensive game, then compute season/last-three averages with distinct covered-game denominators. | No canonical game ID on row; no immutable prior stat versions; missing rows do not prove zero; not an assignment feed. Audit all requested seasons, regular/postseason separation, current source-file status, identity/team joins and per-metric coverage before presenting values. |
| Gridline-derived PBP / team-game data | Candidate corroboration/derivation for game-level offensive events where complete source-game coverage, player identity, and stat definitions are verified. | Full plays are not persisted; existing team aggregates are not enough to invent unretained receiver/rusher/position detail. Any new ingestion is outside this assessment. |
| Existing player red-zone facts | Separate inside-20/10/5 opportunity evidence on only covered games, with its own actual coverage denominator. | Do not blend its partial game coverage into general DvP or interpret absence as zero. Do not claim identical numerator/denominator definitions to whole-game stats. |
| `ffopportunity` `weekly` / `pbp_pass` / `pbp_rush` | Optional, explicitly labeled expected-fantasy-points-per-opportunity analysis after license/attribution review, season and player-ID reconciliation, model version pinning, source capture, and evaluation of the exact fields. | Not needed for the requested raw production-allowed definition; not a CB/defender assignment feed; expected-points values are model outputs, not observed stats or a projection of future opponent outcomes. “Latest” is mutable; release cadence has no guaranteed SLA. |
| Open Source Football WR1 article | Research example of one auditable retrospective receiver-rank-versus-defense EPA analysis. | No figure/text reuse under the article’s noncommercial terms; full-season targeted-air-yard rank is not cutoff safe for pregame use; no corner assignment evidence. |
| nflverse participation / FTN charting | Excluded from current-season pregame DvP. | Participation is season-end/postseason-release historical evidence, not upcoming-week evidence; distinct CC BY-SA attribution terms apply to FTN participation from 2023 onward. Neither is proof of an exclusive one-on-one matchup. |

## Recommendation

Approve only a **descriptive completed-game defense-vs-position** report based on source-backed player-game production with verified complete-game coverage, stable identity and canonical schedule joins, unique covered-game denominators, explicit update time/season/weeks, and no guessed zeros. Keep regular and postseason samples distinct, label small samples, separate general and red-zone coverage, and exclude source rows that cannot be uniquely and chronologically reconciled.

Do not present a named WR–CB/TE–LB pairing, a future “WR1” identity derived from that season’s full results, or an exact historical pregame replay from today’s mutable imports. `ffopportunity` may be separately evaluated as an EP/opportunity supplement—not as the primary Defense vs Position source. No implementation or publication is approved by this research.
