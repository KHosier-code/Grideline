Proceed with Phase 4 of Gridline: MODEL TRAINING AND COMPARISON.

The purpose of this phase is to train and compare statistically valid NFL prediction models using the existing `pregame-v3` feature set.

Do NOT optimize specifically to hit a requested 58%, 60%, or other target win rate.

Do NOT use random train/test splits as the primary validation method.

Use chronological walk-forward testing.

BUILD THREE SEPARATE MODEL FAMILIES

1. Spread / ATS
2. Moneyline
3. Game totals

Do not combine these into one model.

---

## SPREAD MODEL

Build two related outputs:

A. Projected game spread

Example:
Model: KC -5.4
Market: KC -3

B. Probability of covering a specific sportsbook spread

Example:
KC -3 cover probability: 58.7%

Targets must be built from historical final scores.

Do not use post-kickoff information as predictors.

Compare candidate algorithms including:

* Linear regression for projected margin baseline
* Logistic regression for cover probability
* Random Forest
* Gradient Boosting
* XGBoost or equivalent boosted-tree implementation if available

Use regularization and reasonable hyperparameter ranges.

Do not excessively tune to historical betting results.

---

## MONEYLINE MODEL

Predict:

* home win probability
* away win probability

Candidate models:

* Logistic Regression baseline
* Random Forest
* Gradient Boosting
* XGBoost if available

Evaluate probability quality, not just winners.

Metrics should include:

* accuracy
* log loss
* Brier score
* calibration

---

## TOTALS MODEL

Build:

A. Projected game total

Example:
Model total: 49.8

B. Probability of Over/Under against a specific sportsbook total.

Candidate models:

* Linear regression baseline
* Random Forest regression
* Gradient Boosting regression
* XGBoost regression if available

For Over/Under probabilities, either:

* build a calibrated classification layer around market totals, or
* derive probabilities from validated residual distributions

Document which approach is used.

## WALK-FORWARD VALIDATION

Use chronological validation.

Example structure:

Train:
2021

Test:
2022

Then:

Train:
2021–2022

Test:
2023

Then:

Train:
2021–2023

Test:
2024

Then:

Train:
2021–2024

Test:
2025

2026 should remain a true current-season/out-of-sample evaluation period wherever possible.

Do not train a historical test season using that season's future games.

Within-season expanding-window tests are encouraged.

---

## MARKET DATA RULES

Market odds may only be used when they existed before the prediction timestamp.

For historical seasons where Gridline does not possess reliable sportsbook snapshots:

Do NOT fabricate historical DraftKings or FanDuel lines.

Separate:

MODEL QUALITY
from
BETTING PERFORMANCE

Model-quality testing can use:

* actual score margin
* win/loss
* actual total

Betting-performance metrics such as ATS win rate, ROI and CLV require legitimate historical market lines.

If historical sportsbook data is insufficient, explicitly say so.

Do not infer betting results from today's odds.

---

## FEATURE SELECTION

Begin with `pregame-v3`.

Exclude:

* IDs
* final scores
* future results
* post-kickoff fields
* data timestamps that accidentally encode outcomes
* any leakage-prone variables

Create feature importance analysis.

For linear/logistic models show standardized coefficient influence.

For tree/boosted models show feature importance and, if practical, SHAP-style explanations.

Do not automatically delete a feature merely because its initial importance is low.

## LOW-SAMPLE HANDLING

There are currently 564 low-sample feature rows.

Test at least two approaches:

A. Include them with low-sample flags.
B. Exclude/restrict very low-sample observations.

Compare performance.

Do not quietly drop early-season games.

Document whichever approach performs better and why.
